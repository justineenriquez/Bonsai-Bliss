Guide to bonsai styles

Explore the traditional forms that define bonsai aesthetics. Each style is a distinct approach to shaping a miniature tree, from upright and formal to cascading and naturalistic. Knowing them deepens your appreciation for the intention behind every tree.

  • Broom
  • Formal upright
  • Informal upright
  • Cascade
  • Literati

Broom style

Hokidachi

A straight trunk rises to a point where its branches fan out evenly in every direction, forming a rounded canopy like an upturned broom. It suits fine-twigged deciduous trees, and looks especially striking in winter when the branch structure shows.

Formal upright

Chokkan

A common style found in nature, where trees grow in full light without competition. It features a straight trunk with clear tapering and balanced branches, emphasizing strength and harmony. Keep it in shape with regular pruning and wire guiding.

Informal upright

Moyogi

Informal upright bonsai grows in a gentle S-shape, with branches that curve and a trunk that tapers from thick at the base to thin at the top.

Cascade

Kengai

Cascade bonsai grows in tall pots, starting upright before bending downward at an angle, with branches alternating along the curves.

Literati

Bunjingi

This style grows tall and slender, with branches only at the top, as the tree competes for sunlight in dense forests.
